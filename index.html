Why Wrong

“You got it wrong” is the least useful sentence in education.

A red X tells a student nothing and a teacher less. Why Wrong reads a class’s answer sheets and works out the specific misconception behind each wrong answer, the handful of failure modes the class actually shares, and which of your questions measured anything at all.

Does any of this actually work?

The class is simulated, and that is the point. Every student was given a hidden profile of real misconceptions, and their answers were generated from it. The pipeline never sees that profile. These are the numbers for how much of it came back, computed live on the class above.

Four stages. Two of them are not a language model.

The division of labour is deliberate: a model writes the instrument, because authoring one is knowledge-intensive work it is genuinely good at. Everything downstream is arithmetic, because arithmetic is more reliable and can be checked.

  1. Distractor mapping model-authored

    Every wrong option is tagged with the misconception that produces it. This is how real concept inventories are built — the Force Concept Inventory in physics is the canonical example — and it is what makes a wrong answer informative rather than merely negative. Built-in packs are hand-written; new subjects are generated, then validated against the same structural contract before anything is allowed to run.

  2. Per-student inference no model

    A misconception is latent: you never observe it, only answers it makes more likely. A student who holds one still answers correctly sometimes, and a student who doesn’t still slips onto that option occasionally — so a single item cannot diagnose anyone. Evidence is accumulated across every probing item as a log-odds update.

  3. Class structure no model

    Each student becomes a point in misconception space. Students with no confident misconception are separated out before clustering — they are not a failure mode, they are the students who are fine, and leaving them in drags every centroid toward the origin. The rest are compared by direction rather than magnitude, so a student who expresses a misconception weakly still groups with those who express it strongly.

    The number of groups is chosen by silhouette, filtered by a floor on group size. That floor is a teaching constraint rather than a statistical one: a group of two is not a failure mode you can plan a lesson around, it is two students.

  4. Prescription model-adapted

    Each failure mode gets a reteach aimed at the specific belief, plus verification items. Because those items exist, the loop closes: reteach, re-run, watch the group shrink.

Build an instrument for any subject

Name a topic and a model will write the misconception taxonomy and a distractor-mapped item bank for it. The result is validated against the same structural contract as the built-in subjects, and repaired if it falls short, before any analysis runs.
